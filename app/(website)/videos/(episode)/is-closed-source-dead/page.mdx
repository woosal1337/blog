REA is a free tool that gives an AI coding agent the tools to examine a program when you do not have its source code: Ghidra, IDA, or Hopper for desktop programs, plus readers for Electron apps, websites, Android apps, firmware, and game files. In October, it got more than 25,000 GitHub stars in one day. So, is closed source dead?

No. Licenses and terms of use still apply. Most of what a modern app does runs on a company server, where no local tool can look. And on 24 November, one takedown notice from the publisher of GTA 6 removed 3,007 repositories from GitHub.

What changed is the cost of two things. Reading the code that ships to your computer got cheap: a public project rebuilt Gran Turismo 4, and 30,370 of its 30,874 functions match the original. Building a first version of a rival got cheap too: one team now publishes free, agent-built apps that aim at the jobs of Photoshop, Premiere, Lightroom, Illustrator, Acrobat, Word, and Figma. PhotoCraft, the most advanced, says it is about 45% ready for real work.

The stock market is a separate story. Adobe lost about a third of its value this year, Figma more than half, while the Nasdaq 100 rose about 6%. Most of the Adobe drop came before REA or PhotoCraft got attention. No data shows that either project moved a share price.

My verdict: reading got cheap, building got cheap, finishing did not.
